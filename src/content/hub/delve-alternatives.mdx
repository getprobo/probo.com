## A report is worth the auditor who signs it

A SOC 2 report is not a certificate from a software vendor. It's an opinion signed by an independent audit firm. If that firm can't show a passed peer review, or planned almost no audit time, the report can be rejected by the customer you needed it for.

That's why the cheapest all-in quote is rarely the cheapest outcome. Here's [how to evaluate SOC 2 report quality](/hub/evaluate-soc2-report-quality).

## A legitimate auditor, chosen for you or brought by you

Most founders have never hired an audit firm, and you don't have to become an expert. Probo recommends legitimate firms we know, chosen for your company: your size, your framework, your customers. Your compliance officer books the audit with you and handles the back and forth.

If you already have an auditor, you can keep them. That's a real difference with most vendors, who only want to work with you if you go with their partner audit firm.

It's worth asking why. If a compliance program is solid, it should pass with any serious, independent audit firm. A vendor that only works with its own partner is asking you to trust that the audit would go the same way somewhere else.

Keeping your auditor doesn't mean Probo works with any auditor. Before working with a firm, we run an auditor assessment to make sure it's legitimate:

- **Who signs the report,** and where the firm is based.
- **Peer review.** For SOC 2, the firm's AICPA peer review letter, verified on the AICPA's public file.
- **Accreditation.** For ISO 27001, the certification body's accreditation (UKAS, COFRAC or equivalent), verified on the accreditor's site.
- **Audit time.** How many audit days are planned. Too few means nobody is really testing.

We do this because we don't want to support a race to the bottom in SOC 2 audits. A report that nobody tested helps no one, starting with you.

Either way, you pay the auditor directly, at cost. Probo takes no margin on the audit.

## Tailored compliance, not a template

A legitimate auditor tests what your company actually does. So the program has to describe your company, not a generic one.

Template-based tools hand the same policies and the same control set to every customer. But a 5-person startup working remotely on personal laptops can't be expected to check the same boxes as a 200-person company with several offices. Their risks are different, so their controls should be too.

At Probo, a dedicated compliance officer builds your program from a risk analysis of your company and your stack, writes your policies for you, collects the evidence with you and sits with you during the audit. Then they run your compliance all year: access reviews, offboarding checks, policy renewals.

## Already have a report obtained through Delve?

We don't take an existing report at face value, least of all one signed by an auditor who can't show a passed peer review. Here's what usually happens:

1. **We review your existing documentation.** Policies, risk assessment, third-party list, plans, evidence.
2. **We check it against reality, on calls with your team.** Are these policies and controls what you really do day to day? Same for your risks, your third parties and your plans, such as incident response and business continuity.
3. **We keep what holds up and rewrite the rest.** Anything templated that doesn't describe how your company works is rewritten for you.
4. **You pick an auditor you can verify.** One we recommend, or yours after the assessment.
5. **The audit is done again, properly.** With an engagement letter you can show your customers on day one, so deals don't wait for the final report.

Moving your documents into Probo takes about a week. The review calls are where the real work happens.

## Five questions to ask any vendor

1. **Who signs the report?** Get the firm's name before you sign anything.
2. **Who chooses the auditor,** and can I use another one? If not, why?
3. **Can I see the auditor's peer review letter or accreditation,** and verify it myself?
4. **Are the policies written for my company,** or templates?
5. **Does the vendor take a margin on the audit?**
