A customer asked for ISO 27001, and before anything else you want one number. Most pages answer "$15,000 to $80,000, it depends". That range is useless for a budget.

Here is what startups pay in 2026, line by line, and what makes each line move.

## Key takeaways

- **A startup under 15 people pays about 15,000 to 16,000 euros in year one, audit included.** Then about 12,000 euros a year.
- **There are three lines.** The audit, the people who do the work, and the platform. Any quote that hides one of them is not comparable.
- **The audit price depends on the certification body.** About 4,000 to 5,500 pounds with the UK-accredited body most of our customers use. About 7,000 to 12,000 euros with a French COFRAC-accredited body.
- **Years two and three cost less.** Surveillance audits are about half the price of the first audit. Year four resets.
- **An audit under about 5,000 euros is a warning sign.** It cannot pay for the days the standard requires.

## The three lines of the bill

| Line                | What it pays for                                                              | Who you pay                                      |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Certification audit | Stage 1 and stage 2 audits, then the certificate                              | An accredited certification body                 |
| People              | Risk analysis, policies, Statement of Applicability, internal audit, evidence | Your team, a consultant, or a compliance officer |
| Platform            | Where policies, risks, vendors, evidence and tasks live all year              | A compliance software vendor                     |

Most "ISO 27001 cost" figures add the three together, or quote only one. Ask for each line separately.

## What the ISO 27001 audit costs

The audit is done by a certification body, which must itself be accredited for ISO 27001 (UKAS in the UK, COFRAC in France). The price depends mostly on which body you choose and how many people are in scope.

| Certification body                                     | Year-one audit, small company  | How it runs                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| UK-accredited body (the one most of our customers use) | about £4,000 to £5,500         | Remote, quote within hours, dates within weeks |
| French COFRAC-accredited body                          | about €7,000 to €12,000        | On site, lead times of three to six months     |
| Large international certification brand                | more, for the same certificate | Worth it only when a customer names the brand  |

Both a UK and a French certificate are valid everywhere, because the accreditation bodies recognise each other. One price is not "the market price". Ask two bodies for a quote and compare the number of audit days, not only the total.

What moves the audit price:

- **Headcount in scope.** Auditors price in days, and the days follow the number of people.
- **On site or remote.** An on-site audit adds travel and time.
- **The scope.** One product and one team costs less than the whole company with several sites.
- **The brand.** A well-known logo costs more. Most buyers check the accreditation, not the logo.

One thing does not move it: how ready you are. The auditor charges for time.

Before you sign with any certification body, run the checks in [how to check your auditor](/hub/how-to-check-your-auditor). It takes 20 minutes.

## Why a very cheap audit is a red flag

An ISO 27001 audit is paid in days, and the number of days is not up to the auditor. The standard that certification bodies follow sets a minimum audit time based on the people in scope.

For a company under ten people, the starting point is about five audit days. A real first audit rarely goes under 3.5 days. So a quote under about 5,000 euros usually means fewer days than the standard expects, or a body that is not accredited.

A certificate from a non-accredited body is the expensive kind of cheap. Banks, enterprise buyers and public tenders reject it, and you pay for a second audit.

## What it costs year by year

The certificate lasts three years. You do not pay the full audit every year.

| Year | Audit                                | Audit price                       |
| ---- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------- |
| 1    | Certification: stage 1, then stage 2 | Full price                        |
| 2    | Surveillance audit                   | About half of year one            |
| 3    | Surveillance audit                   | About half of year one            |
| 4    | Recertification                      | Close to the year-one price again |

In year one, the audit comes in two stages. Stage 1 is a review of your documentation. Then comes a pause, usually at least two weeks, to fix what the auditor raised. Stage 2 is the audit itself. Both stages are in the year-one price. For the calendar, see [how long ISO 27001 takes](/blog/2025-10-12-how-long-for-iso27001).

A surveillance audit is shorter. The auditor checks that the management system is still running: the internal audit happened, the management review happened, risks were reviewed, and last year's findings were closed.

## What the preparation costs

This is the line that varies the most, because there are three ways to do it.

|                   | Do it yourself with a tool                 | Consultant plus a tool          | Probo                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Who does the work | Your CTO and team                          | A consultant, billed by the day | A dedicated compliance officer           |
| Your time         | 40 to 100 hours                            | Less, plus coordination         | 5 to 10 hours                            |
| Software          | A yearly subscription, often per framework | A yearly subscription on top    | Included                                 |
| What you pay      | The tool, plus the hours                   | Day rates, plus the tool        | From $10,000 a year, platform and people |

The first column looks cheapest and often is not. ISO 27001 asks for documents a tool does not write for you: the scope, the risk analysis, the Statement of Applicability for the 93 Annex A controls, the internal audit and the management review. Somebody has to write them and keep them alive.

The usual trap is paying for a consultant and a tool that do not talk to each other. You end up with two sets of documents. Pick one model before you start.

## Costs that are not on the quote

**A penetration test.** The standard does not require one, but some auditors and many customers expect it ([the detail is here](/blog/2025-10-23-do-you-need-pen-test-for-iso27001)). An automated pentest costs about $1,000 to $2,000 and a manual one about $5,000 to $6,000. Keep it as a separate line.

**An on-site audit.** If your certification body audits on site, add travel and a day of your team in a room.

**A second framework.** If a customer says "ISO 27001 and SOC 2", ask which one they accept before you budget two audits. Most of the work is shared. The audit is not. [SOC 2 vs ISO 27001](/blog/2025-10-08-soc2-or-iso27001) explains how to ask.

**HDS, for French health data.** HDS sits on top of ISO 27001 and needs a French auditor. Doing both audits together costs less than doing them one after the other.

## Does ISO 27001 cost more than SOC 2?

Year one is about the same. See [what SOC 2 costs](/blog/2025-03-04-what-is-soc2-cost) for the numbers.

The difference shows after that. A SOC 2 Type 2 report covers a period, so it is a full audit every year. ISO 27001 is a full audit in year one, two lighter surveillance audits, then a recertification. Over three years, ISO 27001 usually costs less in audit fees.

Do not choose on price, though. Choose the one your customer accepts. US buyers usually ask for SOC 2. Banks, insurers and European buyers usually ask for ISO 27001.

## Five questions before you set a budget

1. **Who is asking?** A named customer with a deal and a date, or "we should probably have it"? If nobody asked, wait.
2. **Which certification body, and is it accredited?** Check it on the accreditor's register.
3. **How many audit days are planned,** and when are stage 1 and stage 2?
4. **Who does the work?** Decide between your team, a consultant and a compliance officer before you buy anything.
5. **What is in scope?** A narrow, honest scope is cheaper to audit and easier to defend.
