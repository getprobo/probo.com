A SOC 2 report or an ISO 27001 certificate is worth the auditor who signs it. Your customer's security team knows that, and the first thing they read is the name on the opinion.

You can run the same check they do before you pay anything. It takes about 20 minutes, and you do not need to know anything about audit firms.

## Key takeaways

- **Start with one question: who signs?** Get the legal name of the firm before you sign with a platform, a consultant or the auditor itself.
- **SOC 2 is signed by a licensed CPA firm.** Ask for its AICPA peer review report and verify it yourself.
- **ISO 27001 is issued by an accredited certification body.** Check the accreditation on the accreditor's own register.
- **Ask how many audit days are planned.** A price that cannot pay for the days is the clearest warning sign.
- **Ask who takes a margin.** If your platform resells the audit, you should know.

## Step 1: get the name of the firm that signs

Everything else depends on this, and it is the question that gets dodged most often.

When the audit is bundled into a platform or a consulting offer, ask: "Which firm signs my report, and can I talk to them before I commit?"

A good answer is a firm name, a country and a contact. "Our partner network" is not an answer. Neither is a name you only get after you have paid.

While you are there, ask who does the fieldwork. In many firms a junior team runs the testing and a partner signs. That is normal. You just want to know the partner exists and reviews the file.

## Step 2 (SOC 2): check the CPA licence and the peer review

Only a licensed CPA firm can sign a SOC 2 report. Two checks cover it.

**The licence.** Look the firm up on its state board of accountancy, or on [CPAverify](https://cpaverify.org), which covers most US states. You are checking that the firm exists, that its licence is active, and that the name matches the one on the report.

**The peer review.** CPA firms that do audit and attestation work are reviewed by another CPA firm every three years. The result is a short report with one of three ratings: pass, pass with deficiencies, or fail.

Ask the firm for three things:

1. Its latest peer review report.
2. The acceptance letter that goes with it.
3. Whether SOC engagements were in the scope of that review.

Then check what you received:

- **The rating is "pass".** "Pass with deficiencies" deserves a question about what the deficiencies were and what changed since.
- **The date is less than three years old.**
- **The firm name matches** the name that will be on your report.
- **The firm appears in the AICPA's public peer review file,** which you can search by firm name on the AICPA peer review site.

A serious firm sends these documents within the day. Hesitation is information.

## Step 2 (ISO 27001): check the accreditation

ISO 27001 works differently. There is no CPA and no peer review. A certification body audits you, and that body is itself supervised by a national accreditation body: UKAS in the UK, COFRAC in France, ANAB in the United States, DAkkS in Germany.

Anyone can issue a document called "ISO 27001 certificate". Only a certificate issued under accreditation is recognised by banks, enterprise buyers and public tenders.

Ask the certification body: "Which accreditation body accredits you for ISO/IEC 27001?"

Then check it yourself:

- **Find the body on the accreditor's public register.** UKAS and COFRAC both publish a searchable list.
- **Check the scope.** The accreditation must cover ISO/IEC 27001 specifically. A body accredited for ISO 9001 only cannot issue an accredited ISO 27001 certificate.
- **Check the certificate will carry the accreditor's mark.** After you are certified, your certificate should also be findable on [IAF CertSearch](https://www.iafcertsearch.org), the global database of accredited certificates.

The country of the certification body does not have to be yours. A UK-accredited certificate is accepted in France and the reverse, because the accreditation bodies recognise each other. What changes between bodies is price, lead time and whether they audit on site. We compare those in [SOC 2 vs ISO 27001](/blog/2025-10-08-soc2-or-iso27001).

## Step 3: ask how many audit days are planned

This is the question that separates a real audit from a rubber stamp, and almost nobody asks it.

An audit is people's time. Somebody has to read your policies, sample your evidence, interview your team and write the report. If the price cannot pay for those days, the days are not happening.

**For ISO 27001,** the number is not up to the auditor. The standard that certification bodies follow sets a minimum audit time based on the number of people in scope. For a company under ten people the starting point is about five audit days, and the reductions an auditor may apply are limited. In practice, a real first audit rarely goes under 3.5 days. A quote under about 5,000 euros usually means fewer days than that.

**For SOC 2,** there is no official table, but the logic is the same. A Type 2 examination samples your controls across at least three months. Ask how many hours or days are budgeted, how many evidence requests to expect, and whether the auditor will talk to your team or only read uploads.

This is why a $1,000 or $2,000 audit is a warning sign. It is not about the price being low. Small peer-reviewed firms are far cheaper than the large audit brands for the same report (see [what SOC 2 costs](/blog/2025-03-04-what-is-soc2-cost)). It is about a price that cannot cover the work.

## Step 4: ask who takes a margin, and who is independent

Two questions for your platform or consultant.

**"Do you take a margin on the audit?"** Some vendors resell the audit with a markup, or get paid for referring you. Neither is illegal. You should just know, because it explains why you are being steered to one firm.

**"Can I use another auditor?"** If the answer is no, ask why. A compliance program that is solid should pass with any serious, independent firm.

Then one question for the auditor: "Did your firm help build the controls you are about to audit?" The firm that designs or implements your controls cannot give an independent opinion on them. Preparation and audit must be two separate parties.

## When to pay for a big audit brand

The large audit firms charge several times what a smaller peer-reviewed firm charges for the same SOC 2 report at startup size.

Pay that when a specific customer requires a specific firm, in writing. It happens with some large enterprises and financial institutions.

Otherwise, most buyers check exactly what this page checks: a licensed, peer-reviewed firm for SOC 2, an accredited body for ISO 27001, and a report that shows real testing. You can see how they read it in [how to evaluate SOC 2 report quality](/hub/evaluate-soc2-report-quality).

## The 20-minute checklist

| Check              | SOC 2                                                        | ISO 27001                                                               |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Who signs          | Legal name of the CPA firm                                   | Legal name of the certification body                                    |
| Right to sign      | Active CPA firm licence (state board or CPAverify)           | Accreditation for ISO/IEC 27001 (UKAS, COFRAC, ANAB or equivalent)      |
| Quality proof      | AICPA peer review report rated "pass", under three years old | Listed on the accreditor's public register, with ISO/IEC 27001 in scope |
| Verify it yourself | AICPA public peer review file                                | Accreditor's register, then IAF CertSearch for the certificate          |
| Audit time         | Hours or days budgeted, number of evidence requests          | Audit days planned against headcount in scope                           |
| Independence       | The firm did not build your controls                         | The body did not consult on your management system                      |
| Money              | Who invoices the audit, and does anyone take a margin        | Same                                                                    |

Send these questions by email. A firm that answers all of them in a day is a firm that will also answer your customer's security team.

## If you already have a report and doubts

Run the same checks on the firm that signed it. If they hold, you are fine.

If they do not, do not wait for a customer to find out. Most of your work can be kept: policies, risk analysis and evidence are checked against what your team really does, and the audit is done again with a firm you can verify. ## Frequently asked questions

### Who can perform a SOC 2 audit?

Only a licensed CPA firm. SOC 2 is an attestation standard of the AICPA, so the report must be signed by a firm of certified public accountants. A compliance platform, a consultant or a security company cannot issue one. They can prepare you, then an independent CPA firm examines your controls and signs the report.

### What is an AICPA peer review?

It is the quality check CPA firms go through. Every three years, another CPA firm reviews the firm's audit and attestation work and issues a report rated pass, pass with deficiencies, or fail. A firm that signs SOC 2 reports should be able to send you its latest peer review report and acceptance letter the same day you ask.

### How do I verify a SOC 2 auditor?

Get the legal name of the firm that signs the report. Check that the firm is a licensed CPA firm on its state board's site or on CPAverify. Ask for the latest AICPA peer review report and acceptance letter, check the rating is a pass, and look the firm up in the AICPA's public peer review file. Then ask how many days of work the audit includes.

### Who can certify a company for ISO 27001?

A certification body accredited for ISO 27001 by a national accreditation body, such as UKAS in the UK, COFRAC in France or ANAB in the United States. Anyone can print an ISO 27001 certificate. Only one issued under accreditation is recognised by banks, enterprise buyers and public tenders.

### How do I verify an ISO 27001 certification body?

Ask which accreditation body accredits it for ISO 27001, then look it up on that accreditor's public register and check that ISO/IEC 27001 is in its scope. Once you are certified, your own certificate should also be findable on IAF CertSearch.

### Is a cheap SOC 2 or ISO 27001 audit a problem?

Not by itself. Small peer-reviewed firms are much cheaper than the large audit brands for the same report. The problem starts when the price cannot pay for the days the work takes. For ISO 27001, a quote under about 5,000 euros usually means fewer than 3.5 audit days, which is below what the standard expects even for a very small company.
