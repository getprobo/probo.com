A prospect's security team asks for your SOC 2 report. You're one signature away from a deal you really want, so you drop the PDF in a Google Drive folder and share the link.

That link now works for anyone who gets it forwarded. You don't know who opened it, nobody signed an NDA, and the report will still be sitting there long after the deal closes (or dies).

Your compliance portal already solves the access problem, but most teams only use it passively: they publish documents and wait for requests. This guide covers the two features that make it active. You can **invite a visitor before they ask**, so they get your documents in minutes under NDA. And you can **post updates** when your compliance changes, so customers hear about your new certification from you instead of finding out at renewal.

If your portal isn't set up yet, start with [how to set up your compliance portal](/blog/2026-10-22-how-to-set-up-your-compliance-portal) and come back.

## Part 1: Give prospects access before they ask

The standard flow is reactive: a visitor finds a restricted document, requests it, someone on your side approves, the visitor signs the NDA, then downloads. It works, but it puts the effort on the prospect. When you're already talking to them, you can skip the waiting.

## Step 1: Upload your NDA

Go to **Compliance portal → Settings → Visitors** ([docs](/docs/product/compliance-portal/visitor-access-and-nda#configure-an-nda)).

![Visitors page with the non-disclosure agreement and the Add visitor button](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/01-visitors-nda.jpg)

If you followed the setup guide, your NDA is already here. If not, upload it now: without an NDA, a visitor you grant access to can download right away, with no confidentiality terms attached.

How the NDA works on Probo: a visitor can request or receive access before signing, but they must **sign the NDA electronically on the portal once their access is accepted, before they can download anything**. Probo keeps the signature status, the completion date, the signing events and a downloadable certificate for each visitor.

Replacing the PDF later only changes the agreement shown in future signing flows. Treat it as a legal change: review the new version and note when it took effect.

## Step 2: Add the visitor

On the same page, select **Add visitor**, type the person's work email and select **Add**.

![Add visitor field with bill@microsoft.com typed in](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/02-add-visitor.jpg)

Use the email of the person who will actually read the report, usually someone on the prospect's security or procurement team. If several people need it, add each of them. Access is per person, which is what gives you a clean record of who saw what.

## Step 3: Grant only the documents they need

Open the new visitor. You'll see their NDA status and, under **Restricted Document Accesses**, every restricted document, audit report and file on your portal, each with **Grant** and **Reject**.

![Visitor detail with NDA awaiting signature and Grant or Reject on each restricted document](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/03-grant-documents.jpg)

Select **Grant** for what this prospect needs, then save. For most security reviews that's your latest audit report (the SOC 2 report or the ISO 27001 certificate and Statement of Applicability) and maybe one or two policies they asked about.

Resist the urge to grant everything. A prospect who asked for a SOC 2 report doesn't need your business continuity plan. Every extra document is one more thing that leaves your control once it's downloaded.

## Step 4: Let the email do the rest

Probo emails the visitor that they've been granted access to your compliance portal, with an **Access Compliance Page** button.

![Access granted email with the Access Compliance Page button](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/04-access-email.jpg)

From there, the visitor opens the portal, signs your NDA (their status in the console moves from **Awaiting signature** to signed) and downloads the documents you granted. Until they first open the portal, they show as **Not visited**, which tells you whether it's worth a nudge.

No DocuSign envelope, no countersigned PDF to chase, no Drive link floating around.

When the deal closes or stalls, revisit the visitor. Revoke grants they no longer need, or deactivate the visitor entirely. Revoking removes portal access, but it can't recall a copy they already downloaded, which is exactly why you granted only what they needed in step 3.

## Part 2: Keep customers posted when your compliance changes

Compliance isn't a one-time event. You renew your SOC 2, you get ISO 27001, you switch from one cloud provider to another, you add an AI provider that processes customer data. Your customers care about all of that, and some of them are contractually owed a heads-up about new subprocessors.

Most teams handle this with a one-off email from someone's inbox, or not at all. Portal updates give you a proper channel: a news feed on your portal, plus an email to everyone who subscribed.

## Step 5: Set your reply-to email

Go to **Compliance portal → Updates** ([docs](/docs/product/compliance-portal/updates-and-mailing-list)). The **Settings** card at the top has one field: **Reply-to email**.

![Updates page with the reply-to email setting and the Subscribers tab](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/05-reply-to-subscribers.jpg)

When a subscriber replies to one of your updates, their reply goes to this address. Use a shared inbox your security team actually reads, like `security@yourcompany.com`, not a personal address or a no-reply.

## Step 6: Build your subscriber list

People join your mailing list in two ways.

**1. They subscribe themselves.** A visitor signs in to your portal and selects **Subscribe to updates**. The dialog states they'll receive compliance updates from your company and can unsubscribe at any time.

![Subscribe to updates dialog with an email address field](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/06-subscribe.jpg)

**2. You add them.** In **Compliance portal → Updates → Subscribers**, select **Add subscriber** and enter their email. Use it for a customer who asked to be kept informed, for example in a security review or a contract negotiation. Only add people who have a reason to hear from you.

Either way, every new subscriber gets a **Confirm your subscription** email and must select **Confirm subscription** before they receive anything. They then land on a **Subscription confirmed** page, and their status in the subscriber list moves from **Pending** to **Confirmed**.

![Confirm your subscription email and Subscription confirmed page](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/07-confirm.jpg)

This double opt-in is a feature, not friction. It proves the person asked for your emails, and it keeps typos and forwarded addresses off your list.

## Step 7: Write the update

On the **Updates** tab, select **Add update** and write a title and a body. It's saved as a **Draft**, with **Send**, edit and delete actions next to it.

![Updates list with a draft update and Send, edit and delete actions](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/08-draft.jpg)

Good reasons to post an update:

- **A new certification or a renewed audit.** "We're now ISO 27001 certified" is the classic.
- **A new or changed subprocessor.** Especially anything that processes customer data: a new cloud region, a new email provider, an AI provider.
- **A completed penetration test**, and where customers can request the summary.
- **A meaningful change to how you handle data**, like a new retention period or a new data residency option.

Keep it factual. Say what changed, what it covers and where to find the evidence. If the certificate or report is on your portal, say it's available on request there, so the update drives people to the document under NDA instead of attaching it.

Before sending, reread the draft for anything confidential or customer-specific. It goes to every confirmed subscriber.

## Step 8: Send it

Select **Send**. Probo shows the **Send update to subscribers** confirmation with the exact title and body that will go out.

![Send update to subscribers confirmation with the full announcement](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/09-send.jpg)

Sending can't be undone. Once sent, the update is read-only: if you spot a mistake, publish a new update with the correction rather than hoping nobody noticed.

The update is emailed to every confirmed subscriber and appears in the **Updates** section of your portal, where anyone visiting can read it, subscribed or not.

![Public compliance portal Updates page listing the ISO 27001 announcement](/blog/compliance-portal-visitors-and-updates/10-portal-updates.jpg)

Over time, that page becomes a timeline of your security program. For a prospect doing due diligence, a portal that shows a renewed audit last quarter and a pentest last month says more than any badge.

## Treat your portal as a channel, not a folder

A compliance portal that never changes looks like a folder someone set up once. One where you invite the right people and post what changed looks like a security team that's paying attention.
