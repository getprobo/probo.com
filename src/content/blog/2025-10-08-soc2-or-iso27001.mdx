A customer's security questionnaire says "SOC 2 or ISO 27001". Or worse, "SOC 2 and ISO 27001". You searched for the difference and found ten articles explaining what an ISMS is.

You do not need the theory first. You need to know which one to start, and whether you really have to do both.

Here is the answer we give founders on a call.

## Key takeaways

- **Get the one your customer accepts.** SOC 2 for US buyers. ISO 27001 for banks, insurers, Europe and most international deals.
- **Ask before you do both.** Under 200 people, almost none of the companies we work with run both. When a customer lists the two, one is usually enough.
- **The work overlaps 70% to 90%. The audit does not.** A second framework reuses most of what you built, then costs a full second audit.
- **Speed depends on the auditor, not the framework.** Ask for audit dates before you sign with anyone.

## SOC 2 vs ISO 27001 in one table

|                   | SOC 2                                                    | ISO 27001                                                             |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What you get      | An attestation report, shared with customers under NDA   | A certificate you can show publicly                                   |
| Who issues it     | A licensed CPA firm, under AICPA standards               | An accredited certification body (UKAS in the UK, COFRAC in France)   |
| Who asks for it   | US customers, mostly software and SaaS buyers            | Banks, insurers, European and international buyers, public tenders    |
| What it examines  | Your controls against the Trust Services Criteria        | Your security management system, plus the 93 Annex A controls         |
| How long it lasts | A Type 2 report covers a period, so you renew every year | Three years, with a lighter surveillance audit in years two and three |
| Time to first one | About four months for a Type 2                           | Two to three months with an auditor who has availability              |

Two things in this table matter more than the rest: who asks for it, and how it renews.

A SOC 2 report is a document your customer's security team reads. An ISO 27001 certificate is a one-page proof that an accredited body checked you. Buyers in the US are used to the first. Buyers almost everywhere else are used to the second.

## The only question that decides it

Do not pick a framework by comparing them. Pick it by asking the customer.

Send one line to the person who sent the questionnaire: "We are starting ISO 27001 (or SOC 2). Would that be accepted for this contract?"

Three things happen when founders send that email.

Most of the time the answer is yes. The questionnaire listed both because it is a template, not because procurement needs both.

Sometimes the answer names one framework. Good, now you know which one to start, and you have it in writing.

Rarely, both are required. Then you sequence them (see below) and you price the deal knowing it.

If nobody has asked yet, you are early. Read [do you need a SOC 2 report](/blog/2026-04-22-do-you-need-a-soc-2-report) before spending anything.

## A decision table for when you cannot ask

| Your situation                                             | Start with | Why                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Most customers are US companies                            | SOC 2      | It is what their security teams expect, and many contracts name it          |
| You sell to banks, insurers or large European accounts     | ISO 27001  | Their evaluation grids ask for a certificate from an accredited body        |
| Customers are split between the US and Europe              | ISO 27001  | It travels better. Many US buyers accept it, few European buyers read SOC 2 |
| A US enterprise contract names "SOC 2 Type 2" specifically | SOC 2      | Procurement will not swap it, so do not argue                               |
| A deal closes in under three months                        | ISO 27001  | No fixed observation period, if the auditor has dates                       |
| You are answering a public tender                          | ISO 27001  | Tenders ask for certificates, not reports                                   |

We made the same call for Probo. We hold ISO 27001 only, and most of our business is in the US. It has not cost us a deal.

## How much of the work is the same

About 70% to 90%, depending on your scope.

Both frameworks want the same foundations: a risk assessment, access control and access reviews, vendor reviews, incident response, onboarding and offboarding, backups, and policies people have actually read.

ISO 27001 adds the management system around those controls:

- a written scope for what is covered,
- a Statement of Applicability, which says for each of the 93 Annex A controls whether it applies to you and why,
- an internal audit before the real one,
- a management review, where leadership signs off on the program.

SOC 2 goes deeper on evidence. A Type 2 auditor samples how each control operated across the whole observation period, so a missed access review in month two shows up in the report. (If you are weighing the two report types, here is [SOC 2 Type 1 vs Type 2](/hub/soc2-type1-vs-type2).)

So the work overlaps. The audit does not. Each framework has its own auditor, its own fee and its own calendar. That is why "let's just do both" doubles the part of the bill you cannot share.

## Auditors and timelines are where they really differ

On paper, both take a few months. In practice the calendar depends on who audits you.

**SOC 2.** A CPA firm examines you. For a Type 2, the observation period is at least three months, so the report arrives about four months after you start. What changes between firms is mostly price and reputation. See [what SOC 2 costs](/blog/2025-03-04-what-is-soc2-cost) and [how long SOC 2 takes](/blog/2025-10-09-how-long-for-soc2).

**ISO 27001.** A certification body audits you in two stages: a documentation review, then the audit itself. There is no fixed observation period, so the auditor's availability sets the pace. In our experience the gap between bodies is large. The UK-accredited auditors we work with quote within hours and give audit dates within weeks, and audit remotely. French COFRAC-accredited bodies often have lead times of three to six months and audit on site. Both certificates are valid. One of them can miss your customer's deadline.

Whichever framework you choose, ask three things before you sign: who signs the report or certificate, where you can verify their accreditation or peer review, and what dates they can commit to. Your buyer's security team will run the same check, and you can see how they read a report in [how to evaluate a SOC 2 report](/hub/evaluate-soc2-report-quality).

The renewal is different too. SOC 2 is a full audit every year. ISO 27001 is a full audit in year one, lighter surveillance audits in years two and three, then a recertification in year four. For the numbers, see [ISO 27001 certification cost](/hub/iso27001-certification-cost) and [how long ISO 27001 takes](/blog/2025-10-12-how-long-for-iso27001).

## If you truly need both

It happens, usually later: you won the US market with SOC 2 and a European bank now wants a certificate, or the reverse.

Do them one after the other, never at the same time on a first program. Get the first one done, keep the controls running, then add the second on the same foundations. The second framework is mostly a mapping and documentation exercise plus a second audit.

If you already hold a SOC 2 Type 2, we wrote the detail of what carries over in [ISO 27001 after SOC 2](/hub/iso-27001-after-soc-2-the-30-percent-shortcut).

You can see each path in practice: [Ahrefs got ISO 27001 in three months](/stories/ahrefs-iso), and [Vybe went for SOC 2](/stories/vybe-soc2) to sell in the US.

## The bottom line

SOC 2 and ISO 27001 prove roughly the same thing to different audiences. Pick the one the customer in front of you accepts, confirm it in one email, and do the second only when a signed contract requires it.
