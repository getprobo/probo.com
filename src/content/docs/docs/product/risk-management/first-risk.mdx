A useful risk entry explains what could happen, why it matters, and how the organization responds. Start with one real exposure rather than filling the register with generic risks.

This guide covers the risk register. Use a [threat-based assessment](/docs/product/risk-management#threat-based-assessments) when you need to model systems, boundaries, threats, and scenarios in more detail.

## Before you start

- Work in the organization that owns the risk.
- Have permission to create or update its compliance records. See [Roles and permissions](/docs/product/roles-and-permissions).
- Gather the context: the affected activity, the unwanted event, its consequence, and the safeguards already in place.

## Build the risk entry

<Steps>

1. **Describe a specific exposure**

   Create a risk with a clear name and category. Use its description to explain the event and consequence. Add assumptions or supporting context in the notes.

   For example: “A former employee retains access to a production service, allowing unauthorized changes or disclosure.” This is more useful than a name such as “Access risk” on its own.

2. **Connect the safeguards**

   Link the internal controls that reduce or monitor the risk. Reuse controls already operated by your team instead of creating a second copy for the same safeguard.

   Link relevant documents and obligations so reviewers can trace the decision to its supporting material. See [Compliance program](/docs/product/compliance-program) and [Assets and obligations](/docs/product/assets-and-obligations).

3. **Separate what exists from what remains to do**

   Record the current situation accurately. A planned safeguard is not evidence that it is operating.

   Create a [task](/docs/product/tasks/overview) for follow-up work. Give it an assignee and, when needed, a deadline. Link the task to the internal control it implements.

4. **Review the entry with the people responsible**

   Confirm the description, assumptions, linked controls, and supporting documents. Use the same assessment method across your program so the reasoning behind your decisions stays comparable.

5. **Keep the register current**

   Update the risk when systems, vendors, controls, or business assumptions change. If you need a reviewed snapshot, publish the risk register as a document version. Publication records a point in time; the live register continues to evolve.

</Steps>

## Check the result

Before treating the entry as ready for review, make sure:

- Someone unfamiliar with the issue can understand the event and consequence.
- The linked controls describe the safeguards your team actually operates.
- Supporting documents explain the context rather than merely repeat the risk name.
- Missing work is recorded as work to do, with a person responsible for it.
- Changes to the underlying systems will trigger a review of the entry.

## When you need a deeper assessment

Use a structured risk assessment for analysis that needs scopes, nodes, boundaries, processes, threats, and scenarios. Link its scenarios to risk-register entries so the analysis and ongoing treatment remain connected without becoming duplicate records.

The [risk-management overview](/docs/product/risk-management) explains this distinction and links to the CLI, MCP, and n8n operations available for automation.
